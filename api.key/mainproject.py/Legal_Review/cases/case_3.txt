FICTIONAL CASE 3
Rivera v. Summit Group
Supreme Court of the Fictional State of Columbia
27 Col. 415, 522 P.3d 710 (2023)

NOTICE: This is an invented case and citation. It has no real-world legal authority.

FACTS:
Rivera, age 59, was denied a promotion. During a meeting several months before the decision, a supervisor referred to Rivera as “approaching retirement.” The supervisor did not make the final promotion decision.

RULE:
An age-related remark may be evidence relevant to whether an employer acted with discriminatory intent. A stray remark, standing alone, does not automatically establish liability. Its significance depends on context, timing, the speaker's role in the decision, and other evidence.

HOLDING:
The court vacated summary judgment and remanded because the remark, considered with evidence that the supervisor influenced the decision, could contribute to a finding of pretext. The court expressly declined to hold that one age-related remark automatically establishes liability.
