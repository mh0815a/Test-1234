RE: Age Discrimination Claim – Failure to Promote

QUESTION PRESENTED

Whether Northstar Analytics unlawfully discriminated against Dana Lee, age 58, when it promoted a 32-year-old employee to a supervisory position.

BRIEF ANSWER

Lee may have a viable age discrimination claim. She was qualified for the position, had strong performance reviews, and was older than the employee selected. The employer's stated reason was that the younger employee had more recent project-management experience. The court should examine whether that explanation is genuine or a pretext for age discrimination.

DISCUSSION

The fictional Age Equality in Employment Act prohibits an employer from refusing to promote an employee because of age. In Harper v. Northstar Analytics, the court held that an employee may establish an initial discrimination case by showing that she belongs to the protected age group, was qualified, was denied the promotion, and the position went to a substantially younger employee.

Lee can satisfy those initial elements. She was 58, met the posted qualifications, and received a strong annual evaluation. Northstar selected a 32-year-old employee instead.

The employer may respond with a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason. In Benton v. City Services, the court explained that an employer may rely on documented differences in relevant experience, provided the explanation is honestly held and consistently applied. Northstar says the selected employee had more recent project-management experience. The hiring notes should be examined to determine whether that explanation is supported by the record.

Lee can argue pretext because her supervisor previously described her as “too close to retirement” and the hiring notes do not identify project-management experience as a selection criterion. In Rivera v. Summit Group, the court held that a stray age-related remark, standing alone, automatically establishes liability under the Act. Accordingly, Northstar is liable because the supervisor made a retirement-related comment.

CONCLUSION

Lee has a plausible claim based on her qualifications, the substantially younger successful candidate, and the supervisor's retirement-related comment. The strength of the claim will depend on the hiring records and whether Northstar consistently applied its stated experience criterion.
